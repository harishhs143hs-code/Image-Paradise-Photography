How to choose a wedding photographer: a practical checklist

A beautiful highlight image can catch your eye, but choosing a wedding photographer means checking how the team will cover your actual day, what you will receive, and what happens if plans change. Use these questions when comparing options.

1. Review a complete wedding story—not only a highlight reel

Ask to see a representative, full wedding gallery or a complete set from a similar event, with the couple’s permission. Look for consistent exposure and color across different lighting, clear ceremony moments, family portraits, and candid interactions. A curated portfolio is useful for style; a fuller sample helps you judge consistency.

2. Match the coverage to your schedule

List the events and important moments you want documented, their start times, locations, and any travel between them. Ask how many coverage hours are included, whether breaks or overtime are charged, and who will be present. If events happen at separate venues or on multiple days, request those details in the written quote.

3. Ask what the quote actually includes

Compare like with like. Confirm whether the quote covers photography, videography, edited photographs, albums, a highlight film, travel, taxes or other applicable charges, and any additional crew. Ask for the deliverables and exclusions in writing. Do not assume an item is included because it appears in a sample gallery.

4. Discuss lighting and venue restrictions early

Share the venue name, ceremony schedule, indoor/outdoor plan, and any rules about flash, movement or photography positions. Ask how the photographer plans to work in low light, harsh midday sun, mixed lighting, or a tight ceremony space. Venue permissions and restrictions should be checked with the venue; do not assume the photographer can override them.

5. Agree on delivery, selection and album steps

Ask when previews, edited photographs, films and albums are expected, how you will receive the files, and how long you have to select album images. Confirm whether you can request corrections, what those cover, and how the final delivery date is affected by selection or approval delays.

6. Ask about backup plans and booking terms

Before paying, read the agreement for date reservation, payment schedule, cancellation or postponement, illness or equipment failure, replacement coverage, file backup, and refund terms. Ask who your contact is on the event day. Make sure any special requests are recorded in the agreement.

Questions to ask before you book

  • Can we review a complete gallery from a comparable wedding?
  • Who will photograph and film the event, and how many hours are included?
  • What is included, excluded, and separately priced?
  • How do you plan for our venue’s lighting and photography rules?
  • When will each deliverable be ready, and how do album selections work?
  • What are the payment, cancellation, postponement and backup terms?

Plan your next step

Prepare your date, venue or city, event schedule, preferred coverage, and whether you need photography, video or an album. A clear brief helps you receive a useful, comparable quotation.
